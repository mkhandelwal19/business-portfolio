Websites for jewellers and goldsmiths

In this trade the website is not selling jewellery. It is selling the reason to walk into your shop rather than a chain store.

Trust is the product, and hallmarking is how you prove it

A customer choosing a jeweller is weighing one risk above all others: am I getting the purity I am paying for? The chains have spent enormous sums answering that question. An independent jeweller answers it with BIS hallmarking and purity stated per piece — and then, usually, forgets to put it on the website.

Metal purity on every piece, hallmarking shown plainly, certification for stones. That is the site.

What we have learned building these

The things that decide whether a site in this trade works, which are rarely the things a brochure talks about.

  • 01

    Never publish a live gold rate you cannot maintain

    It is the most requested feature and the most dangerous one. A stale rate on your website is a customer arguing at your counter. Link to a source or leave it off — do not hand-maintain a number that moves daily.

  • 02

    Purity belongs on the piece, not in a footnote

    18K, 22K, 24K, per item. A catalogue where purity is a general statement reads as evasive, whether or not it is.

  • 03

    Making charges are the question nobody publishes

    You do not have to give a number. Saying how they are calculated — per gram, percentage, flat — removes the fear that stops people enquiring.

  • 04

    Bridal runs on a different timeline

    Months, not minutes, and usually several people deciding. Lead times per piece matter more than a buy button.

BIS hallmarking

Hallmarking is mandatory for gold jewellery in the categories it covers, and customers increasingly know to look for it. We show the HUID and purity alongside the piece rather than burying it in a general trust statement, because a specific mark on a specific item is the thing that actually reassures.

Questions we get from this trade

Can we show the live gold rate?
We would advise against hand-maintaining one. A rate that is a day old is worse than no rate, because a customer arrives expecting it. If you want one, it should pull from a source automatically or link out to one — never be a number someone has to remember to update.
Should customers be able to buy online?
Rarely worth it at this value. People buying gold want to see and weigh it. The site earns its money by getting a qualified person into the shop with a specific piece in mind, which is a much easier thing to build and to trust.
We make to order. How does that work on a website?
Each piece carries an indicative lead time, and the enquiry captures what they want, for when. Bridal especially — someone planning a wedding needs to know if eight weeks is realistic, and that answer on the page saves a dozen calls.
