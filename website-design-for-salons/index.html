Websites for salons, spas and beauty parlours

There is one reason people open a salon website, and most salon websites refuse to answer it.

They came to find out what it costs

Not to admire the design. Someone is deciding between three salons at eleven at night, and they are not going to ring any of them to ask the price of a haircut.

A salon that publishes its price list beats one that says "call for pricing", every time, and it is not close. The service menu is the page that carries the whole site: every service, a starting price, and how long it takes.

What we have learned building these

The things that decide whether a site in this trade works, which are rarely the things a brochure talks about.

  • 01

    Starting prices are enough — you do not need exact ones

    "Haircut from ₹400" answers the question without committing you on a client whose hair takes two hours. "Call for pricing" answers nothing and loses the booking.

  • 02

    Duration matters as much as price

    People are fitting you into a day. A service list that says how long each thing takes gets booked more than one that does not.

  • 03

    Bridal is a different business on the same site

    Bridal packages carry a different price bracket, a different decision timeline and usually a different decision-maker. It deserves its own page, not a line in the price list.

  • 04

    Your own work, not stock models

    Prospective clients are judging whether your work looks like what they want. Stock photography of a model in a studio tells them nothing about you.

What you can and cannot claim

Treatment claims — permanent results, guaranteed fairness, medical-sounding outcomes — are a genuine risk in this trade, and the ones that sound most persuasive are usually the ones you cannot make. We write the service list so it sells on specificity and price rather than on promises nobody can keep.

Questions we get from this trade

We do not want to publish prices. Competitors will see them.
They already know. Competitors can walk in, or ring and ask, and they do. The person your hidden price list actually stops is the customer deciding at eleven at night — and she picks the salon that told her.
Our prices change. Will the site be wrong?
Starting prices change slowly. Send us the new list on WhatsApp and it is updated the same day during your support period. That is far less work than the phone calls a missing price list generates.
Can clients book and pay online?
Booking, yes. Taking payment upfront is possible but rarely worth it for a salon — no-shows are better handled by a confirmation message than by holding someone money. If you do want payments, that is the store build.
