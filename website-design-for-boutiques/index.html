Websites for boutiques, labels and saree houses

There is a question to settle before a single page is designed, and it decides the entire build.

Do you sell online, or does the site get people into the shop?

These are two different websites and it is worth being honest about which one you want.

If the site sells, you need a cart, checkout, GST invoicing, returns and a payment gateway in your name — that is the store build. If the site is a lookbook, its job is to make the cloth desirable and move someone into the shop or a WhatsApp conversation. Most boutiques want the second and get sold the first.

What we have learned building these

The things that decide whether a site in this trade works, which are rarely the things a brochure talks about.

  • 01

    The weaver and the craft carry the value

    Where the cloth comes from, who wove it, how long it took, what the motif means. This is what separates a boutique from a rack, and it is what almost no boutique website says.

  • 02

    Made-to-measure needs a real enquiry flow

    If you alter or make to order, the site has to capture measurements and timelines properly, or every enquiry becomes six WhatsApp messages.

  • 03

    Fabric photographs badly on a phone in a shop

    Drape, weight and true colour need daylight and a plain wall. This is worth one afternoon of doing properly, and it is the single biggest lever on a boutique site.

  • 04

    Sizing honesty prevents returns

    Actual measurements, not just S/M/L. It reads as confidence and it saves the conversation later.

If you take money, four legal pages become mandatory

The moment a site transacts, Indian rules require terms and conditions, a privacy policy, a refund and cancellation policy and a shipping policy — and your payment gateway will check for them before approving the account. If you only want a lookbook, none of this applies, which is one more reason to settle the question first.

Questions we get from this trade

Should we sell online or not?
Ask what you want to happen after someone finds you. If the answer is "they come to the shop and we show them things", a lookbook converts better and costs less to run. If you are genuinely shipping orders and have the stock discipline for it, the store build is the right one. We will say which we think fits after seeing your stock.
We have hundreds of pieces. Do they all go on the site?
No. A lookbook works by editing. Twenty pieces photographed well will sell more than three hundred shot badly, and a catalogue you cannot keep current does more harm than a small one you can.
Can you photograph the clothes?
We are a web studio, not a photography one, so we do not shoot for you — but we will give you the exact shot list, the lighting conditions and the framing that works, and most owners get usable results on a phone in an afternoon.
