Websites for restaurants, cafes and cloud kitchens

A restaurant website has one job before it has any other. Most of them get it wrong, and it is not a design problem.

People come to read the menu and find out if you are open

That is it. That is the whole visit, for the overwhelming majority of people who land on a restaurant site. They are hungry, they are deciding between you and two others, and they are on a phone.

So the menu is one tap from anywhere on the site, and the opening hours are exact — not "open daily", but the actual times, including the afternoon break. A beautiful site where the menu is a downloadable PDF has failed. We have seen that PDF cost more bookings than a bad logo ever did.

What we have learned building these

The things that decide whether a site in this trade works, which are rarely the things a brochure talks about.

  • 01

    Veg, non-veg, Jain and egg markers are not optional

    In this market they decide whether someone orders at all. If the physical menu card does not mark them, that is a conversation to have before the site goes live, not after.

  • 02

    The dish people order twice becomes the hero

    Not the most expensive one, and not "everything". One photograph of the thing regulars come back for does more than a gallery of twelve.

  • 03

    Real photos beat stock, every time

    Customers are checking whether the place looks like somewhere they want to sit. Stock food photography reads as a chain, and reads as hiding something.

  • 04

    Swiggy and Zomato links belong on the site

    Sending someone to an aggregator looks like a loss, but the alternative is them searching for you there anyway and finding a competitor first.

FSSAI licence number

Your FSSAI licence number has to be on display, and the website counts. We put it in the footer of every page as standard. It costs nothing, it is legally required, and a customer who looks for it and finds it trusts the rest of the page more.

Questions we get from this trade

Our menu changes every day. Can the website keep up?
Yes. A daily board is a normal thing to build for — a section you send us on WhatsApp each morning, or that you edit yourself. Seasonal and festival menus work the same way. What does not work is a PDF that gets replaced once a year.
Do we need a booking system?
Usually not. Most restaurants of this size take bookings by phone or WhatsApp and answer them faster than any form. We build the booking flow you will actually use. If you genuinely hold tables and need a form with times, that is the Premium tier.
We are only on Swiggy and Zomato. Do we need a website at all?
The aggregators own your customer relationship and take a cut of every order. A website does not replace them — it means someone searching your name finds you, sees your full menu at your prices, and can order directly. The two work together.
