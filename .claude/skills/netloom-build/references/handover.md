# Handover

The site says *"you own everything"* and *"never locked in."* This document
is how that stops being a slogan.

---

## What the client owns, and where it must live

| Thing | Must be in | Never in |
|---|---|---|
| Domain name | Their registrar account, their card | Ours |
| GitHub repo | Transferred to their account, or they are an admin | Ours alone |
| Domain email (Zoho) | Their Zoho account | Ours |
| Razorpay account *(store)* | Their name, their KYC, their bank | Ours — this is also illegal |
| Supabase project *(store)* | Their account | Ours |
| Google Business Profile | Their Google account, us as manager | Ours as owner |
| Photographs | Theirs, or licensed, with `LICENCES.md` to prove it | Unrecorded |

If any of these is in a Netloom account at handover, it is a lock-in whether
we intended one or not, and it makes the promise on the homepage untrue.

**We may hold manager or collaborator access.** We must not hold ownership.

---

## The handover pack

Send as one WhatsApp message with attachments, and email a copy so it is
findable in a year.

1. **The live URL**, and the URL of the GitHub repo.
2. **A credentials sheet.** Every account, what it is for, and who owns it.
   Never send passwords in the same message as the account list; call and
   read them, or use the registrar's own invite flow.
3. **`LICENCES.md`** — where every photograph came from. If they are ever
   challenged, this is their defence.
4. **A one-page "how to change things"** — see below.
5. **The invoice**, with GST if applicable.
6. **Care plan terms**, if they took one, with the start date.

---

## The one-pager: how to change things

Write this per client, in plain language, in the language they read. It
should cover exactly four things and nothing else:

- **How to change your opening hours.** The single most common request, and
  it changes at every festival.
- **How to add a photo.**
- **How to change a price.**
- **Who to call, and what the response time is.**

Do not write them a Git tutorial. Most of these clients will WhatsApp you the
change, which is fine and is what the care plan is for. The point of the
one-pager is that they *could* do it without us — which is what makes the
ownership promise real.

## After go-live

**Day 3** — check it. Is it indexed? Any console errors? Any 404s?

**Week 2** — one message. *"How's it going? Anyone mentioned the site?"*
This is not a sales call. It is how you find out something is broken that
they were too polite to report, and it is how you get a referral.

**Month 2** — send whatever analytics you have, in one sentence they can
understand. *"142 people found you through Google last month, and 31 tapped
the WhatsApp button."* That sentence sells the care plan better than the
care plan does.

---

## Testimonials — the one thing to ask for

Ask for a review **only after something good has happened** — a customer
mentioned the site, an enquiry came through, they got a booking.

And then: netloom.in currently carries the line *"We will not show you
testimonials you cannot verify."* That is the most credible thing on the
site. So when a client gives a testimonial, put it up with their real name,
their real business and a link to it. If they would rather not be named, do
not use it at all. An anonymous testimonial is worth less than no testimonial
on a site that made that promise.
