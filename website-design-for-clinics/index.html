Websites for clinics, dentists and diagnostic labs

A patient looking at your website is asking one question, and it is not about your equipment.

They are checking whether you are real, and whether you are qualified

Someone choosing a clinic is making a decision with more at stake than choosing a restaurant, and they are doing it from a search result with no idea who you are.

The single strongest thing you can put on the page is each practitioner's name, qualification and medical council registration number. It is required anyway. It is also the most convincing sentence on the whole site, and most clinic websites leave it off.

What we have learned building these

The things that decide whether a site in this trade works, which are rarely the things a brochure talks about.

  • 01

    Registration numbers are a trust signal, not red tape

    Name, qualification as they want it written, council registration number, years in practice. A patient who can verify you will choose you over a clinic that only shows a stock photo of a stethoscope.

  • 02

    Hours and directions matter more than the about page

    Someone is deciding whether they can get to you today. Exact timings per practitioner, a working map link, and parking if there is any.

  • 03

    No outcome claims, no before-and-afters where they are restricted

    Medical advertising rules in India are stricter than most web shops know. We write around them rather than build something that has to come down.

  • 04

    The appointment flow should match how you actually work

    Most clinics run on a phone and a register. A form that promises a slot you cannot honour is worse than a clearly displayed number.

Medical council registration and advertising rules

Every practitioner listed carries their council registration number. We also keep the copy inside what the advertising codes permit — no guaranteed outcomes, no comparative claims about other practitioners, and care with testimonials and imagery. This is the part of a clinic site that gets studios in trouble, and it is easier to write correctly the first time than to strip out later.

What the site includes

  • Practitioner profilesName, qualification, registration number, specialisation, availability.
  • Services and conditionsWhat you treat, written so a patient recognises their own problem.
  • Appointment flowPhone, WhatsApp or form — whichever you answer reliably.
  • Maps and exact hoursPer practitioner where they differ, which they usually do.

Questions we get from this trade

Can we show patient testimonials?
Carefully, and sometimes not at all depending on your speciality. Indian medical advertising codes restrict testimonials and outcome claims more than most people expect. We will tell you plainly what is safe for your practice rather than build something that has to be removed.
We have four doctors with different timings. Can the site handle that?
Yes, and it should — it is one of the most common reasons a patient calls. Each practitioner gets their own availability, and the site shows who is in on a given day rather than one blanket set of hours.
Do we need online appointment booking?
Only if you will honour it. Most clinics run on a phone and a register, and a form that issues slots nobody is watching creates angry patients. We build the flow that matches how your front desk actually works.
